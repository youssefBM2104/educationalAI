## Q1  [easy]
According to Eurostat’s beginners guide, who determines the contents of the reference shopping basket whose prices are collected each month across different regions and types of shops, and which is reviewed annually to remain representative?
- A. National statistical offices
- B. Shops and service providers
- C. The European Statistical Agency
- D. Eurostat

**Answer:** A
**Explanation / model answer:** The guide explains that national statistical offices conduct the monthly price surveys across regions and shop types (Items for the shopping basket). The selected items whose prices are surveyed each month make up the reference shopping basket, which is reviewed annually to stay representative (The items must be carefully selected… reviewed each year). Therefore, these national offices determine the basket’s contents.

## Q2  [medium]
In Eurostat’s beginners material on HICP, how do the specifically selected items feed into the monthly data collection, and which entities are actually visited to record those prices?
- A. They are the items in the reference shopping basket, for which prices are collected through price surveys by sampling types of shops rather than visiting specific outlets.
- B. They are the items in the reference shopping basket, for which prices are collected through price surveys by aggregating regional averages rather than visiting specific outlets.
- C. They are the items in the reference shopping basket, for which prices are collected through price surveys by visiting shops and service providers.
- D. They are the items in the reference shopping basket, but prices are collected through price surveys that focus only on the types of shops and regions sampled, not on visiting shops or service providers.

**Answer:** C
**Explanation / model answer:** The guide explains that carefully selected items form the reference shopping basket (chunk #17). Each month, national statistical offices conduct price surveys to collect the prices of those basket items by visiting shops and service providers across different regions and types of shops (chunk #18). Hence, the items feed into the reference basket, and the surveys involve in‑person collection at shops and service providers.

## Q3  [hard]
In a market economy, an unexpectedly poor harvest reduces wheat supply, and the price of bread sold in shops is 10% higher than in the reference period. According to Eurostat’s beginners material, which statement best explains how this change becomes part of the EU’s official inflation figure?
- A. Supply-and-demand conditions make prices for goods vary; those prices are the consumer prices households pay; the HICP uses these consumer prices and, by comparing the latest with the reference period, turns the 10% rise into a higher index value (e.g., around 110) that feeds into the published inflation rate.
- B. A 10% rise in bread prices would be recorded by the HICP as a deflation rate rather than an increase, because HICP focuses on price decreases for goods.
- C. Market conditions can change prices, but the HICP records only the procedural stages of calculation; the 10% rise would be noted as a step in the process rather than as an inflation rate.
- D. Only services respond to market supply and demand, so because consumer prices are limited to services, a 10% rise in bread prices would be excluded from the HICP.

**Answer:** A
**Explanation / model answer:** Eurostat’s introduction explains that in a market economy, supply and demand drive price changes (price variability) for goods and services that consumers buy. Those are consumer prices. The HICP is a harmonised measure based on consumer prices and is used to compare inflation across EU countries. Once prices are collected, the calculation compares the latest prices to a reference period; a 10% increase corresponds to an index around 110, contributing to the reported inflation rate.
