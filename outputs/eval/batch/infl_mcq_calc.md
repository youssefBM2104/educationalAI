## Q1  [easy]
A national statistical office has selected a representative set of goods and services for a reference shopping basket and collected their prices each month across different regions and shop types. To produce a price index for a product category that can be compared over time, what should they do next with those collected prices?
- A. Compare the latest collected prices with the previous (reference) prices for the basket items and convert the changes into index values (for example, a 10% increase becomes 110 and a 5% decrease becomes 95).
- B. Adjust the collected prices for supply and demand conditions to estimate the index without using past prices as a reference.
- C. Base the index on wholesale, import, and producer prices for the basket items rather than on consumer prices.
- D. Replace the basket items every month to reflect current promotions and report the index simply as the current euro price level of the basket without comparing to a prior period.

**Answer:** A
**Explanation / model answer:** The shopping basket provides the representative items whose consumer prices are recorded monthly from different regions and types of shops. To calculate the price index, the latest prices are compared with previous (reference) prices and expressed as index values (e.g., +10% → 110; −5% → 95). This links the role of the basket (item selection and monthly price collection) with the actual index calculation method.

## Q2  [medium]
A country’s national statistical office has just reviewed its reference shopping basket to reflect current spending and then collected monthly prices for those items across regions and shop types. Which statement correctly links the office’s role to how those data are used and what indexed value results if a representative item’s price is 10% higher than in the reference period?
- A. The national statistical office primarily conducts price surveys across regions, and this regional coverage alone determines the measure; a 10% rise is shown directly as 10% without converting to an index value.
- B. The national statistical office defines the reference shopping basket, whose collected prices are then used in the price index calculation; a 10% rise relative to the reference period corresponds to an index value of 110.
- C. The national statistical office defines the reference shopping basket mainly to illustrate the range of shops and service providers surveyed, not for the calculation itself, so no index value such as 110 is derived.
- D. The national statistical office defines the reference shopping basket, which is used in the price index calculation; however, a 10% rise relative to the reference period is reflected by an index value of 95.

**Answer:** B
**Explanation / model answer:** Per the guidance, national statistical offices select a representative set of items to form the reference shopping basket (chunk #17). Monthly prices for these items are collected across regions and shop types (chunk #18) and then used in the price index calculation, where percentage changes relative to the reference period are converted to index values (chunk #20). A 10% increase corresponds to an indexed value of 110.

## Q3  [hard]
An EU analyst is preparing the official HICP figure for October. According to the harmonised approach, which description correctly links the multi-stage calculation to the final monthly number?
- A. A multi-stage calculation applies directly to individual consumer prices, so the HICP is reported for specific items instead of as a month-specific inflation rate.
- B. A multi-stage calculation produces the harmonised index of consumer prices, which defines the inflation rate, and that rate is then reported for the specific month.
- C. A multi-stage calculation produces the harmonised index of consumer prices, which defines the inflation rate, and that rate is assigned to the reference month rather than the current month.
- D. The stages do not involve the HICP; they apply directly to the inflation rate, which defines the general price level rather than a specific month.

**Answer:** B
**Explanation / model answer:** Eurostat’s HICP is produced through a defined, harmonised process with several stages of calculation (collecting prices and computing indices). The HICP framework defines the inflation rate, and this rate is published for particular months (e.g., October). This is supported by the description of HICP as a harmonised method with monthly publication and by the index calculation steps comparing current-month prices to a reference period.
